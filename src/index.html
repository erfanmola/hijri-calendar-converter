About the Hijri calendar

The Hijri calendar is a lunar calendar of twelve months, and its year is about eleven days shorter than the Gregorian year. That is why Islamic dates such as Ramadan and the two Eids move earlier each Gregorian year. Year 1 of the calendar begins with the Hijrah, the Prophet Muhammad's migration from Makkah to Madinah.

How the dates are calculated

This widget uses the standard tabular (arithmetic) Hijri calendar, a fixed 30-year cycle with leap years at set positions. Many countries instead start each month from a local moon sighting or an official calendar such as Umm al-Qura, so the date announced where you live can differ by a day. For religious observances, follow your local authority's announcement.

Frequently asked questions

What is today's Hijri date?

The widget above shows today's date in the tabular Hijri calendar. Your local moon-sighting announcement may differ by a day.

Why does my country's Islamic date sometimes differ by one day?

Many countries begin each month from a local moon sighting or an official calendar, while this widget uses a fixed arithmetic calendar. Both are legitimate; use your local announcement for religious dates.

How many days are in a Hijri year?

A Hijri year has 354 or 355 days, about eleven days fewer than a Gregorian year.

Can I convert a Gregorian date to Hijri?

Yes. Use the converter in the widget, or the full Hijri calendar on QiblaFind, which converts in both directions.
